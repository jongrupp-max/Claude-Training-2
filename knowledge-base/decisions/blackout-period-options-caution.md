# Blackout Period Options Issuance Caution

**Date:** 2026-04
**Category:** client-handling

## Summary

Carta established guidance that companies in regulatory or transactional flux (de facto "blackout periods") should generally wait before issuing options. If the company insists on proceeding, a 409A should use scenario analysis with heavy documentation of risks and caveats, and the company should discuss the timing with counsel.

## Context

In April 2026, a company was considering issuing options while in the middle of a complicated situation — regulatory issues had emerged immediately after signing an LOI, the situation was in flux, and even the client acknowledged the company's value was diminishing by the day. Rizwan Ahmed raised concern in #ramp-services-questions that this constituted a de facto blackout period. The risk: options granted at an inflated value during a blackout period could create tax exposure for optionees.

## What Was Decided

- Companies with material unresolved events (regulatory issues, LOI pending resolution, significant value uncertainty) should be advised to wait before issuing options
- If the company wants to proceed anyway, Carta can proceed with a 409A using scenario analysis with heavy documentation of risks, assumptions, and caveats
- Carta cannot advise on corporate decisions (whether to issue options now) — that's a legal/tax question for counsel
- Analysts should leave notes in Val Station for context when proceeding under unusual circumstances like this
