# Crypto Volatility Selection: Coinbase Anchor

**Date:** 2026-03
**Category:** methodology

## Summary

For crypto-adjacent companies requiring volatility selection in their 409A, Carta decided to anchor to Coinbase (approximately 90% volatility) rather than crypto miners (MARA, Riot, Argo, BTCS) or pure token-backed companies (Circle), on the basis that the latter reflect commodity/crypto asset exposure rather than company fundamentals.

## Context

A crypto-adjacent company's 409A required volatility selection for a comparable company analysis. The prior 409A had used 90% volatility, consistent with Coinbase. In March 2026, the question arose in #advisory-services-questions as to whether the current 409A should maintain that selection or shift to other crypto-sector comps. Additionally, the team encountered a related question about why the 409A volatility and common warrant valuation volatility didn't match — a separate but connected issue.

## What Was Decided

- For crypto-adjacent companies whose business model resembles an exchange or fintech (not a crypto miner or token issuer), Coinbase is the preferred volatility anchor
- Crypto miners and pure token-backed companies reflect commodity/crypto asset exposure and are therefore less comparable for 409A purposes
- Volatility of ~90% should be maintained consistent with prior 409As when the Coinbase comp remains the best available
- Divergence between 409A volatility and warrant valuation volatility can be explained by the different risk profiles: common share class implied vol in the 409A approximates the warrant valuation's selected vol
