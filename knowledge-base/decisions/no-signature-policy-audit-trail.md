# No-Signature Policy & Digital Audit Trail

**Date:** 2026-06
**Category:** client-handling

## Summary

Carta codified its official position that 409A reports are not signed by any individual. Instead, the report is finalized when the company's board/management accepts it within the Carta platform, creating a secure digital audit trail. Carta provides audit defense support as a corporate entity, making a physical signature unnecessary for safe harbor status.

## Context

Multiple situations in 2026 surfaced clients or prospects requiring signed 409A reports — a PE-backed portfolio company (via Ariana Nurisso in #val-sales, Jun 2026), an IRA transfer situation (see [IRA Transfer Custom Engagement](ira-transfer-custom-engagement.md)), and a client concerned about audit acceptance. Rizwan Ahmed drafted a comprehensive, reusable response that the team could deploy across all these situations. Previously, responses had been ad-hoc; this established a standard playbook.

## What Was Decided

- Carta does not sign 409A reports — this is policy, not a case-by-case decision
- The report is finalized via board/management acceptance within the Carta platform, creating a digital audit trail
- Carta stands behind its valuations as a corporate entity and provides audit defense support, making a signature unnecessary
- This official position is to be used as the standard response to all "can you sign the 409A?" requests
- For clients with a hard requirement for a physical signature (e.g. IRA custodian requirements), a custom engagement may be explored separately
