Carta's sales motion is performing well: the business closed $20.1M in revenue over the past 12 months at an 88.6% win rate, with a healthy $56M open pipeline and $18.3M in late-stage deals ready to convert. Cap Table leads ARR by volume while Fund Admin and Fund Tax carry the highest per-deal values—a duality that should inform where capacity is invested. Collections present a mixed picture: 80% of invoiced revenue has been collected, but $4.3M in cancellations and $2.2M in slow-moving open AR warrant active follow-up to protect the revenue already won.
Section 1 of 3
Sales Pipeline Health
The pipeline is both active and selective. An 88.6% win rate on 255 closed opportunities (226 won, 29 lost) points to a well-qualified funnel — reps are working the right deals. The $56M open pipeline provides strong forward coverage, with Discovery ($12.4M) and Contract Signed ($10.5M) representing the two largest stages. The primary loss driver is No Decision — eight deals lost not to a competitor but to organizational inertia — suggesting the biggest lever for improving win rate is coaching reps to create urgency earlier in the sales cycle, not outcompeting on price or features. Note: closed-won data is dense through November 2025; Q1 and Q2 2026 figures reflect early-closing deals only, as most opportunities with 2026 close dates remain active in the pipeline.

ARR by Product & Customer Type
Two distinct growth motions are running in parallel. The first is a high-volume, moderate-ACV motion anchored in Cap Table and 409A — sold primarily to Corporations and LLCs — that generates deal count and market penetration. The second is a high-ACV enterprise motion in Fund Admin and Fund Tax — sold to LPs, VC Firms, and PE Firms — that generates outsized revenue per deal. Fund Admin to VC Firms averages $124K ACV across 14 deals, the highest-value repeatable segment in the book. The strategic question is whether to treat these as independent sales motions or as a land-and-expand sequence: win on Cap Table, then upsell into Fund products as the customer grows.

Invoice Collections
The overall collection rate is solid at 80% — $42.5M collected of $52.9M invoiced — but two signals require attention. First, $2.2M of open AR is aged 61–180 days and needs active outreach to prevent migration into bad debt. Second, $4.3M in cancelled invoices (8.1% of total billed) is unusually large: at this magnitude the cause matters, whether that's billing errors, contract disputes, or credit memos, each pointing to a different remediation. A positive note: there is currently zero 180+ day exposure in open (Posted) invoices — the live AR book is cleaner than historical patterns suggest it once was.

Key Takeaways
  • 1
    The sales motion is working — protect the win rate, fix the "No Decision" leakage. An 88.6% win rate with $20.1M won in 12 months is strong. The only structural gap is 8 deals lost to indecision — a coaching and urgency problem, not a product or pricing problem. Addressing it could convert 2–3 additional deals per quarter at the current average deal size.
  • 2
    Fund Admin × VC Firms is the highest-return capacity investment. At $124K average ACV across 14 proven deals, this segment has more than double the per-deal value of the Cap Table motion. Allocating dedicated headcount here — or building a playbook from the 14 wins — would likely generate disproportionate ARR relative to rep count.
  • 3
    Cap Table + 409A form a durable volume base that may be an upsell channel. Corporations and LLCs buying Cap Table (110 deals) and 409A (48 deals) represent a substantial installed base. If any of these customers are growing into fund structures, there's a natural expansion path into Fund Admin, Fund Tax, and QSBS — worth mapping against the customer list.
  • 4
    Open AR is manageable but needs immediate attention in the 61–180 day bucket. $2.2M of the $3.6M open AR is already in the slow-pay zone. Proactive outreach now — before any of it ages past 180 days — is the highest-leverage collections action available to the team this quarter.
  • 5
    The $4.3M cancellation figure needs a root-cause audit before the next planning cycle. At 8.1% of total invoiced value, cancellations are large enough to materially affect revenue recognition and DSO metrics. Understanding whether the driver is billing process errors, contract disputes, or customer churn will determine whether the fix sits in Billing, Legal, or Customer Success.
